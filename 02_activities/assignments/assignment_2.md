      ```
      Based on the 3 important qualities of data visualization from slide decks 1 and 4, I felt this was a relatively good visualization compared to other options on the website.
      
      Substantive: The visualization presents the data accurately and honestly in a fairly objective way, by providing both the raw counts and bar format. It also does not hide what the total N would be or when there are missing values. Additionally, it lists the author and date last updated. The only thing that may be missing is the data source.
      
      Perceptual: It is very easy to understand what data are being presented as the breakdown of each year and grouping is shown in 2 different ways. All variables and groupings are clearly labelled, and the colour scheme can be changed to be high contrast.
      
      Aesthetic: It is visually pleasing enough, but not the best. Considering the substantive and perceptual strength, I think the relative weakness of aesthetic can be overlooked. It is visually easy to identify relevant aspects of the data.
      ```

    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```
      Using the criteria again from slide decks 1 and 4, I felt this was a poor visualization. 
      
      Perceptual: I think this is the largest problem, as it is difficult to integrate what everything means at a quick glance. I think this is largely due to the fact that they have plotted 4 different variables on the same graph and hovering over certain circles or numbers in the visualization will white out or cover other data. It is not inherently intuitive and the instructions for reading the chart are very small and easily missed. 

      Substantive: I do not think this is a neutral or honest representation of the data. The circle size seems misleading, especially in the relative scaling of what size means. Additionally, only impressive statistics are enlarged and highlighted and the text explictly states that the purpose of this chart is to celebrate the movie and its legacy (i.e. paint it in a good light).
      
      Aesthetic: Contrary to the "good" visualization above, this one is most certainly much more aesthetically pleasing, although the font size is even more imbalanced than the good example. The only complaint would be the lack of contrast in the colours, as it does not seem like a particularly colour-blind friendly palette.           
      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      I think the fidelity of data is most important to improve here. For example, having more representative scaling of circles or colours. The colour gradient for the IMDb rating also does not make much sense for a unipolar scale (i.e. randomly shifting colours in the middle rather than going from light to dark of the same shade or vice versa). All the important text for chart interpretation (e.g. axis, legend) are much too small while other less useful text is much too large. Additionally, it would be prudent to choose a palette with more contrast that may be more colorblind friendly.

      Fundamentally, I would have also recommended making more than 1 visualization for all of these parameters (e.g. release year, film count, IMDb rating, and IMDb Vote) rather than squishing them all into this one chart. I would have also tried to explain what some of these parameters are or represent (e.g. what is an IMDb Vote and what does it represent) for the naive reader.
      ```
